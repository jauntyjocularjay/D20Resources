## Evolution Of Robotics

Progress Level 5: The widely accepted dawn of robotics, beginning with the programmable manipulator arm in 1954. Early robots are crude automata used in military, scientific, and specialized industrial roles; simple programmable robots and toy automata are available. Humanoid and animal-shaped robots exist but are distinct from true androids.

Progress Level 6: Robots grow more versatile and begin to make choices from multiple options. Common components such as hands and legs appear more frequently; emergency services and industry use specialized robots. Household smart appliances and more humanlike bipedal frames become feasible.

Progress Level 7: Robots are ubiquitous across many sectors. Electroflex artificial-muscle technology enables more lifelike androids. Bioreplicas (synthetic lookalikes) appear in limited use and raise legal and social questions; synthetic pets and animal bioreplicas remain common.

Progress Level 8: Advanced metallurgy and materials enable liquid-state robots that can alter their form. These are mostly used in exploration, with military interest.

## Robots As Heroes

Two anthropomorphic robot types can be played as heroes: the biodroid (often called an "android") and the bioreplica (a synthetic). Neither is truly sentient, but both possess artificial intelligence that allows independent decision-making and learning. Biodroids appear at PL 6+, while bioreplicas are products of PL 7.

A biodroid or bioreplica hero begins play as a 1st-level Strong, Fast, Tough, Smart, Dedicated, or Charismatic hero. Unlike ordinary robots, biodroid and bioreplica heroes advance in levels like human characters and may take levels in advanced classes where eligible.

Links to the playable robot pages are below; detailed rules are provided in their sections.

### Biodroid

Biodroids (often called "androids") are construct robots modeled after a biological species. They are playable in settings of Progress Level 6 or higher and may emulate virtually any living creature (except oozes and plants) in movement and behavior.

#### Biodroid Traits

---

- **Size:** Same as the emulated species; only Small and Medium biodroids may be selected as player characters.
- **Speed:** Same as the emulated species (base 30 ft for human biodroids).
- **Ability Scores:** A heroic biodroid has no Constitution score and a starting Charisma score of 5. Other ability scores are determined normally (including any emulated species modifiers). Biodroids may improve mental abilities (Intelligence, Wisdom, Charisma) with level advancement but not physical abilities (Strength, Dexterity).
- **Starting Occupation:** Biodroids do not receive starting occupations.
- **Hit Points:** A biodroid gains 1d10 hit points per level. At 1st level it gets the maximum (10). It does not apply a Constitution modifier to hit points but gains additional HP at 1st level based on size (Small +5, Medium +10).
- **Armor:** A biodroid can wear armor or have integrated armor attached to its frame.
- **Critical Systems:** Although constructs, biodroids have vital areas and are subject to critical hits.
- **Cybernetic Incompatibility:** Biodroids cannot be fitted with cybernetic attachments.
- **Immunities:** Immune to mind-influencing effects, poison, sleep, paralysis, stunning, disease, necromancy effects, and effects requiring a Fortitude save unless the effect also works on objects or is harmless. They are not subject to nonlethal damage, ability damage, ability drain (except as noted under Destruction/Restoration), energy drain, or massive damage effects. They cannot be raised from the dead.
- **Lifelike Appearance:** Distinguishing a biodroid from its emulated species requires a successful Spot check (DC 10); Disguise can raise that DC.
- **Manipulators:** Manipulators resemble organic digits and function like organic counterparts.
- **Rejuvenation Cycle:** A biodroid must shut down 8 hours within each 24‑hour period to replenish energy. Failing to do so imposes a cumulative –1 penalty to attack rolls, ability checks, skill checks, and saves per day until fully recharged.
- **Repairable:** Cannot heal naturally; Repair skill (DC 30) heals 1d10 damage per successful hour of work.
- **Robot Resurrection:** A biodroid reduced to 0 HP is destroyed and cannot be repaired as a whole; its brain may be salvaged and installed into another frame (see Robot Resurrection rules).
- **Sensors:** Begins play with a Class IV sensor system.
- **Skills:** Gains and assigns skill points as other nonhuman characters do, and uses its Charisma modifier on Constitution-based skill checks (including Concentration checks).
- **Free Language Skills:** Can read, write, and speak one language.
- **Feats:** Receives no feats at 1st level but gains feats normally as it advances.
- **Action Points:** Gains action points as organic heroes do.
- **Height & Weight:** Same height range as the biological counterpart; weight equals 1.5× the normal weight of the biological counterpart.
- **Level Adjustment:** +0.

### Bioreplica

Bioreplicas ("synthetics") are highly lifelike construct robots available in settings of Progress Level 7 or higher. They are modeled after biological species and built with synthetic sheaths and components to closely simulate flesh and behavior.

#### Bioreplica Traits

---

- **Size:** Same as the emulated species; only Small and Medium bioreplicas may be chosen as player characters.
- **Speed:** Same as the emulated species (base 30 ft for human bioreplicas).
- **Ability Scores:** A heroic bioreplica has no Constitution score and a starting Charisma score of 10. Other ability scores are determined normally. Bioreplicas may improve mental abilities (Int, Wis, Cha) with level but not physical abilities (Str, Dex).
- **Starting Occupation:** Bioreplicas do not receive starting occupations.
- **Hit Points:** A bioreplica gains 1d10 HP per level; at 1st level it gets the maximum (10). No Constitution modifier applies to HP; size grants additional HP at 1st level (Small +5, Medium +10).
- **Armor:** Natural armor bonuses from a modeled creature do not apply.
- **Critical Systems:** Bioreplicas possess vital areas and can suffer critical hits.
- **Cybernetic Incompatibility:** Cannot be fitted with cybernetic attachments.
- **Immunities:** Immune to mind‑influencing effects, poison, sleep, paralysis, stunning, disease, necromancy, and effects requiring a Fortitude save unless also affecting objects or harmless. Not subject to nonlethal damage, ability damage/drain (except as noted), energy drain, or massive damage effects. They cannot be raised from the dead.
- **Lifelike Appearance:** Spot check (DC 20) is required to distinguish a bioreplica from its emulated species; Disguise can alter that DC.
- **Manipulators:** Manipulators resemble organic digits and function like organic counterparts.
- **Rejuvenation Cycle:** Must shut down 8 hours per 24‑hour period to replenish energy; failure causes a cumulative −1 penalty per day to attacks, checks, and saves until recharged.
- **Repairable:** Repair skill (DC 30) heals 1d10 damage per successful hour of repair work.
- **Robot Resurrection:** A bioreplica reduced to 0 HP is destroyed and cannot be repaired whole; its brain may be salvaged and installed in an intact frame.
- **Sensors:** Begins play with a Class VII sensor system.
- **Skills:** Gains and assigns skill points as other nonhuman characters; uses Charisma modifier on Constitution-based skill checks (including Concentration).
- **Free Language Skills:** Can read, write, and speak one language.
- **Feats:** No feats at 1st level; gains feats normally as it advances.
- **Action Points:** Gains action points like organic heroes.
- **Height & Weight:** Same height range as biological counterpart; weight is 1.5× the normal weight of the counterpart.
- **Level Adjustment:** +0.

### Non-Heroic Robots

Most robots are nonheroic constructs built to perform tasks; they do not take class levels. Factory-installed skill and feat software can grant programmed capabilities as part of their design.

#### General Robot Traits

---

- **Hit Die:** d10.
- **Base Attack Bonus:** +3/4 of total Hit Dice.
- **Good Saving Throws:** None.
- **Skill Points:** None (unless provided by programmed skill software).
- **Feats:** None (unless provided by programmed feat software).
- **Starting Occupation:** Robots never get starting occupations.
- **Weapon & Armor Proficiency:** Proficient with their manipulators only; not proficient with armor unless specified.
- **Ability Scores:** Robots have no Constitution score and usually no Intelligence score. Size and frame determine ability scores (see frame tables in the SRD).
- **Extra Hit Points:** Robots gain extra hit points based on size.
- **Manipulators:** Typically two functioning manipulators; Large or larger frames may have more.
- **Immunities:** As constructs, robots are immune to mind‑influencing effects, poison, sleep, paralysis, stunning, disease, necromancy, and effects that require a Fortitude save unless they also work on objects or are harmless. They are not subject to nonlethal damage, ability damage/drain, energy drain, or massive damage effects.
- **Critical Hits:** Robots with armature, biomorph, and liquid‑state frames are not subject to critical hits. Biodroids and bioreplicas, however, have vital systems and can suffer critical hits.
- **Repairable:** Robots cannot heal naturally but can be repaired using the Repair skill (DC 30 heals 1d10 points per hour of successful work).
- **Robot Resurrection:** A robot reduced to 0 HP is destroyed and cannot be repaired as a whole; its brain may be salvaged and installed in a similar but intact frame.
- **Weight:** Generally heavier than an organic creature of similar size by a factor of 1.5.

## Robot Frames

A robot's frame is the basic physical form it takes, including chassis and internal power source. The frame determines base statistics and the base purchase DC (not including accessories such as locomotion, manipulators, armor, sensors, or equipment). Key frame properties include Frame Size, Base Purchase DC, Base Hit Dice, Extra Hit Points, Base Ability Scores, and the Maximum Hit Dice/Purchase DC modifier (how purchase DC increases with extra HD).

Available frame types include Armature, Biodroid, Biomorph, Bioreplica, and Liquid‑State (see subsections below for details).

## Locomotion

Robot locomotion options vary by Progress Level (PL). Below are concise summaries of common locomotion modes (base speed and purchase DC notes).

### Forced Air

[PL 5]

The robot hovers about an inch off the ground by forcing air beneath itself. It handles poorly and moves at half speed over poor surface conditions.

- **Base Speed:** Fly 30 ft (clumsy)
- **Purchase DC:** One-half the base purchase DC of the robot's frame.

[Return to Table of Contents](#table-of-contents)

### Legs (Multiple)

[PL 5]

Three or more mechanical legs allow walking. Moves at half speed on obstructions, stairs, or poor surfaces. Only legged robots can jump.

- **Base Speed:** 30 ft
- **Purchase DC:** One-half the frame's purchase DC.

[PL 5]

Equipped with one or more propellers for water travel; cannot travel on land without another locomotion mode.

- **Purchase DC:** One-quarter the base purchase DC of the robot's frame.

[PL 5]

Equipped with a rotor (like a helicopter). Slower than an air propeller but can hover without falling.

- **Base Speed:** Fly 30 ft (poor)
- **Purchase DC:** One-quarter the frame's purchase DC.

### Stationary

[PL 5]

The robot cannot move (bolted or fixed in place). Usually used for factory robots or fixtures.

- **Base Speed:** 0 ft (cannot be improved)
- **Purchase DC:** Included in robot's base purchase DC (not listed separately).

### Track

[PL 5]

Follows a preset track and cannot deviate. If separated from the track it becomes stationary. Can only maneuver obstacles if the track provides a path.

- **Base Speed:** 10 ft
- **Purchase DC:** One-tenth the frame's base purchase DC (rounded down).

[PL 5]

Equipped with tank-like treads to roll over most terrain; can handle shallow steps but not stairs. Cannot jump or swim.

- **Base Speed:** 20 ft
- **Purchase DC:** One-quarter the frame's base purchase DC.

[PL 5]

Wheeled robots perform well on level ground but move at half speed on poor surface conditions. Cannot jump or swim.

- **Base Speed:** 30 ft
- **Purchase DC:** One-half the frame's base purchase DC.

## Manipulators

Without some kind of manipulating appendage, robots cannot lift or move objects. Manipulators can be as crude as a simple probe or as complex as a five-fingered hand.

 A Medium-size or smaller robot may have up to two functioning manipulators. Larger robots may be equipped with a greater number of functioning manipulators, as determined by their size:

- Robot's Frame Size — Manipulators
- Medium-size or smaller — Up to 2
- Large — Up to 4
- Huge or larger — Up to 8

To build a manipulator from scratch, a character must succeed at a Craft (mechanical) check (DC 25] after investing 24 hours in its construction. A character without a mechanical tool kit takes a −4 penalty on the skill check. The character must also make a Wealth check against the manipulator's purchase DC. Mastercraft robot manipulators can be fashioned using the Mastercrafter feat.

Size: A manipulator, as an object, is usually two size categories smaller than the robot for which it's designed; for example, a hand designed for a Medium-size robot can be considered a Tiny object. A manipulator's size is usually important only for portability and concealment purposes.

Damage: Robots can use their manipulators as melee weapons, dealing piercing, slashing, or bludgeoning damage based on the type of manipulator and the robot's size (see Table: Manipulator Damage). Some types of manipulators deal nonlethal damage only.

Purchase DC: The cost of each manipulator. This cost is always a fraction of the base purchase DC of the robot's frame.

### Claw

[PL 5]

Claws resemble pincers, but the opposed surfaces cover the length of the appendage. Claws suffer the same handicaps as pincers when attempting tasks involving manual dexterity, but they deal greater damage.

- Damage: Lethal slashing or nonlethal bludgeoning.
- Purchase DC: 10 + one-quarter the base purchase DC of the robot's frame.

[Return to Table of Contents](#table-of-contents)

### Jaws

[PL 5]

Only robots modeled after creatures with bite attacks have jaws. Robotic jaws are essentially large clamps with teeth.

- Damage: Lethal piercing.
- Purchase DC: 10 + one-quarter the base purchase DC of the robot's frame.

[Return to Table of Contents](#table-of-contents)

### Pincer

[PL 5]

A pincer is a two-fingered, clawlike appendage that focuses all the grip strength on a pair of opposed surfaces, rather like a pair of tongs. Pincers allow the robot to manipulate and lift objects without much difficulty, but objects specifically made for humanoid hands (like firearms) are usually beyond the pincers' ability to operate. At the GM's discretion, tasks involving manual dexterity suffer a −4 penalty for a robot equipped with pincers.

- Damage: Lethal piercing or nonlethal bludgeoning.
- Purchase DC: 5 + one-quarter the base purchase DC of the robot's frame.

[Return to Table of Contents](#table-of-contents)

### Probe

[PL 5]

Similar to a special-use gripper, a probe is simply an instrument of some kind, meant to measure torque, temperature, or some other factor. If the robot attempts to manipulate or lift an object with a probe, it takes a −4 penalty on the check.

- Damage: Lethal piercing or nonlethal bludgeoning.
- Purchase DC: 5 + one-tenth the base purchase DC of the robot's frame (rounded down).

[Return to Table of Contents](#table-of-contents)

### Special-Use Gripper

[PL 5]

The robot has a manipulator designed for a specific task. When the robot uses a special-use gripper for a task other than its intended task, the robot suffers a −4 penalty on the check if the check involves manual strength or dexterity.

- Damage: Nonlethal bludgeoning only.
- Purchase DC: 5 + one-tenth the base purchase DC of the robot's frame (rounded down).

[Return to Table of Contents](#table-of-contents)

### Hand

[PL 6]

A hand is a step up from a claw, in that it has more digits—usually three or four, total. Hands are a bit more adaptable as well, imposing only a −2 penalty when attempting tasks involving manual dexterity.

- Damage: Nonlethal bludgeoning only.
- Purchase DC: 10 + one-quarter the base purchase DC of the robot's frame.

[Return to Table of Contents](#table-of-contents)

### Advanced Hand

[PL 7]

Advanced hands are essentially the same in structure as human hands, though some might have additional fingers for specialized work. Advanced hands suffer no penalties when attempting tasks involving manual dexterity.

- Damage: Nonlethal bludgeoning only.
- Purchase DC: 10 + one-quarter the base purchase DC of the robot's frame.

[Return to Table of Contents](#table-of-contents)

### Task Hand

[PL 7]

Task hands function just like advanced hands, except that they are equipped with additional joints and often with additional or telescoping digits, enabling them to multitask, as well as to spread the robot's grip strength over a slightly wider area.

A robot with a task hand gains a +1 equipment bonus on Climb, Craft, Demolitions, Disable Device, Drive, Escape Artist, Forgery, Pilot, Repair, Search, Sleight of Hand, and Treat Injury checks. This bonus improves to +2 if the robot has two or more task hands. At the GM's discretion, the robot can gain this bonus when attempting certain Perform checks as well.

- Damage: Nonlethal bludgeoning only.
- Purchase DC: 10 + one-quarter the base purchase DC of the robot's frame.

### Class I Sensor System

## Robot Weapon Rules

A robot can be armed with manipulators, handheld weapons, mounted weapons, or any combination of the three. However, during any given round of attacks, it must choose whether to attack with its manipulators, handheld weapons, or mounted weapons, as it cannot switch between them during the same turn.

Manipulators are considered natural weapons, and using them does not provoke attacks of opportunity.

A robot armed with handheld weapons follows the normal rules of combat, suffering the usual penalties for two-weapon fighting.

A robot armed with multiple mounted weapons gains one primary attack with a mounted weapon of its choice; the rest of its mounted weapons are treated as secondary attacks (–5 penalty on the attack roll). Using a mounted ranged weapon provokes attacks of opportunity.
