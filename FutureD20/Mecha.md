## Core Mecha Systems

### Mecha Body Size

Mecha bodies come in Large, Huge, Gargantuan, and Colossal sizes. A mecha's size determines how many equipment slots (places where weapons and additional equipment can be installed) it has, and how well it measures up in combat.

#### Mecha Size Modifiers

| Modifier | Description |
|---|---|
| Size Modifier | Apply this modifier to the mecha's attack rolls and to its Defense. |
| Equipment Slots | The number of locations where weapons and other mecha equipment can be installed. |
| Hit Points | The mecha's hit points, and the number of hit points the mecha provides its operator. |
| Base Speed | The mecha's base land speed. Certain types of armor reduce base speed (see Mecha Armor). |
| Height | The mecha's height in feet. |
| Weight | The mecha's weight in pounds. |
| Fighting Space | The mecha's fighting space. |
| Reach | The mecha's reach, even without weapons. |
| Purchase DC | The base purchase DC of the mecha without armor, weapons, and other equipment. |
| Restriction | The level of license required to purchase the mecha legally. |

|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---|---|---|---|---:|---|
| Colossal | -8 | 25 | 800 | 60 ft. | 64-128 ft. | 250,000 lb. or more | 30 ft. by 30 ft. | 15 ft. | 60 | Mil (+3) |
| Gargantuan | -4 | 17 | 400 | 50 ft. | 32-64 ft. | 32,000-250,000 lb. | 20 ft. by 20 ft. | 15 ft. | 48 | Mil (+3) |

#### Large Mecha

Essentially big suits of armor, Large mecha excel in urban battlefields and starship boarding actions, where they move through buildings and corridors to find the enemy. Large mecha are the easiest to operate. However, they can't carry the intense array of weapons that bigger mecha can, nor are they as strong or durable.

Combat Statistics: A Large mecha adds a +8 equipment bonus to a character's Strength score. It imposes a -1 size penalty on attack rolls and to Defense. Depending on the material used, a Large mecha has 100 bonus hit points, which are added to the character's total and subtracted first when the character takes damage. It takes a -4 penalty on Hide checks.

A Large mecha has a single slam attack that deals 1d8 points of damage (plus the character's increased Strength modifier). Its reach is 10 feet, and its base speed is 30 feet.

Base Purchase DC: 40

Equipment Slots: A Large mecha has 7 equipment slots available.

| Location | Slots |
|---|---:|
| Helmet | 1 |
| Back | 1 |
| Left arm | 1 |
| Right arm | 1 |
| Shoulders | 1 |
| Torso | 1 |
| Boots | 1 |

#### Huge Mecha

Common weapons on the futuristic battlefield, Huge mecha offer a solid balance between cost, agility, and sheer bulk. They perform well in any combat environment, but they favor locations with some variation in terrain. In the wide-open desert or depths of outer space, they can fall prey to larger mecha.

Combat Statistics: A Huge mecha adds a +16 equipment bonus to a character's Strength score. It imposes a -2 size penalty on attack rolls and to Defense. Depending on the material used, a Huge mecha has 200 bonus hit points, which are added to the character's total and subtracted first when the character takes damage. It takes a -8 penalty on Hide checks.

A Huge mecha has a single slam attack that deals 2d6 points of damage (plus the character's increased Strength modifier). Its reach is 10 feet.

Base Purchase DC: 44

Equipment Slots: A Huge mecha has 11 equipment slots available.

#### Gargantuan Mecha

Gargantuan mecha are highly experimental in PL 6 societies, but quite common at PL 7. Units of this size are titans of the battlefield, able to destroy almost anything they can hit. Gargantuan mecha are common in outer space environments but are often too expensive and unwieldy for planet-based missions.

Combat Statistics: A Gargantuan mecha adds a +24 equipment bonus to a character's Strength score and a -2 penalty to Dexterity. It imposes a -4 size penalty on attack rolls and to Defense. Depending on the material used, a Gargantuan mecha has 400 bonus hit points, which are added to the character's total and subtracted first when the character takes damage. It takes a -12 penalty on Hide checks. A Gargantuan mecha has a single slam attack that deals 2d8 points of damage (plus the character's increased Strength modifier). Its reach is 15 feet.

#### Colossal Mecha

Colossal mecha do not exist in PL 6 societies and are rare sights even at PL 7. Only at PL 8 do they leave the experimental stage and enter the battlefield in respectable numbers. Massive juggernauts, Colossal mecha represent the epitome of futuristic warfare, with firepower sufficient to destroy starships or level entire city blocks. They typically serve as mobile artillery and walking weapon platforms.

Combat Statistics: A Colossal mecha adds a +32 equipment bonus to a character's Strength score and a -4 penalty to Dexterity. It imposes a -8 size penalty on attack rolls and to Defense. Depending on the material used, a Colossal mecha has 800 bonus hit points, which are added to the character's total and subtracted first when the character takes damage. It takes a -16 penalty on Hide checks. A Colossal mecha has a single slam attack that deals 4d6 points of damage (plus the character's increased Strength modifier). Its reach is 15 feet.

### Quadrupedal Mecha

Mecha can be built with four legs instead of two. A quadrupedal mecha sacrifices equipment slots for added speed and stability.

- Its base land speed increases by 10 feet.
- It gains a +4 stability bonus on checks made to resist bull rush and trip attempts.

#### Equipment Slot Changes

| Size | Standard Slots | Quadrupedal Slots | Slots Given Up |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Large | 7 | 6 | Boots |
| Huge | 11 | 8 | Left hand, Right hand, Boots |
| Gargantuan | 17 | 14 | Left hand, Right hand, Boots |
| Colossal | 25 | 22 | Left hand, Right hand, Boots |

### Mecha Superstructure

A mecha's superstructure, including its exoskeleton, interior braces, and other structural parts, can be made from any sufficiently advanced metal alloy. Whatever its composition, a mecha's superstructure has a hardness that reduces the damage the mecha takes from weapons and collisions.

To build a mecha superstructure from scratch, a character must succeed at a Craft (mechanical) check (DC 30) and a Craft (structural) check (DC 30) after investing the required assembly time based on the mecha's size.

| Mecha Size | Assembly Time |
|---|---:|
| Large | 150 hours |
| Huge | 300 hours |
| Gargantuan | 600 hours |
| Colossal | 1,200 hours |

A character without a mechanical tool kit takes a -4 penalty on both skill checks. The character must also make a Wealth check against the mecha's base purchase DC.

#### Superstructure Materials

- Hardness: The amount of damage the material absorbs from a weapon hit or collision.
- Base Purchase DC Modifier: The modifier applied to the mecha's base purchase DC.
- Restriction: Since mecha require a license to own and operate, the materials used for making them do not require special licenses to purchase.

| Superstructure Material | PL | Hardness | Base Purchase DC Modifier | Large | Huge | Gargantuan | Colossal |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Alumisteel | 5 | 10 | -4 | 36 | 40 | 44 | 56 |
| Duralloy | 6 | 15 | 0 | 40 | 44 | 48 | 60 |
| Vanadium | 6 | 20 | +4 | 44 | 48 | 52 | 64 |
| Neovulcanium | 7 | 20 | 0 | 40 | 44 | 48 | 60 |
| Neutronite | 7 | 25 | +4 | 44 | 48 | 52 | 64 |
| Megatanium | 8 | 30 | 0 | 40 | 44 | 48 | 60 |

#### Alumisteel (PL 5)

This easy-to-acquire alloy is lightweight and reasonably strong. Alumisteel can also be used for armor (see Mecha Armor).

- Hardness: 10
- Base Purchase DC Modifier: -4

#### Duralloy (PL 6)

Duralloy is harder, heavier, and more durable than alumisteel. It can also be used to fashion armor (see Mecha Armor).

- Hardness: 15
- Base Purchase DC Modifier: None

#### Vanadium (PL 6)

Vanadium alloy absorbs a respectable amount of damage and is easy to mold.

- Hardness: 20
- Base Purchase DC Modifier: +4

#### Neovulcanium (PL 7)

Similar to duralloy, neovulcanium uses plasma-forging techniques to create an alloy of unparalleled resilience. Neovulcanium can also be used for armor (see Mecha Armor).

- Hardness: 20
- Base Purchase DC Modifier: None

#### Neutronite (PL 7)

Neutronite is a tough steel alloy into which a weave of free neutrons has been pressed. It is extremely resilient but also incredibly massive, weighing approximately five times more than a similar volume of lead.

- Hardness: 25
- Base Purchase DC Modifier: +4

#### Megatanium (PL 8)

Sandwiched layers of crystal carbon and neovulcanium held in a magnetic matrix, megatanium is exceedingly hard and durable. Megatanium can also be used for armor (see Mecha Armor).

- Hardness: 30
- Base Purchase DC Modifier: None

### Mecha Armor

Armor can be welded or otherwise fixed securely to a mecha's superstructure, providing an equipment bonus to the mecha's Defense. Mecha armor does not impose a maximum Dexterity bonus on the mecha operator (as worn armor does) and does not require a special proficiency feat to use.

Installing armor on a mecha requires a Craft (mechanical) check (DC 20). The check is made after investing an amount of time determined by the mecha's size.

| Mecha Size | Installation Time | Removal Time |
|---|---:|---:|
| Large | 3 hours | 1.5 hours |
| Huge | 6 hours | 3 hours |
| Gargantuan | 12 hours | 6 hours |
| Colossal | 24 hours | 12 hours |

Armor can be removed in half the normal installation time with a successful Repair check (DC 20).

#### Armor Statistics

- Equipment Bonus: The equipment bonus the armor provides to the operator's Defense.
- Armor Penalty: Mecha armor applies this penalty to the operator's Balance, Climb, Escape Artist, Hide, Jump, Move Silently, and Tumble checks.
- Speed Penalty: The amount by which the armor reduces the mecha's base speed.
- Purchase DC: The armor's cost.
- Restriction: Since mecha require a license to own and operate, mecha armor does not require a special license to purchase.

| Alumisteel Armor | 5 | +5 | -6 | -5 ft. | 10 + one-half the mecha's base purchase DC |
| Duraplastic Armor | 5 | +3 | -4 | None | 5 + one-half the mecha's base purchase DC |
| Duralloy Armor | 6 | +8 | -8 | -10 ft. | 10 + one-half the mecha's base purchase DC |
| Resilium Armor | 6 | +6 | -5 | None | 5 + one-half the mecha's base purchase DC |
| Crystal Carbon Armor | 7 | +10 | -8 | None | 15 + one-half the mecha's base purchase DC |

#### Alumisteel Armor (PL 5)

This easy-to-acquire alloy is lightweight and reasonably strong. Alumisteel can also be used as a building material for mecha superstructures (see Mecha Superstructure).

- Equipment Bonus: +5
- Armor Penalty: -6
- Speed Penalty: -5 ft.
- Purchase DC: 10 + one-half the mecha's base purchase DC

#### Duraplastic Armor (PL 5)

Duraplastic armor is made of advanced plastic polymers, such as carbon fiber and high-grade fiberglass. Although relatively cheap and light, it does not offer tremendous protection.

- Equipment Bonus: +3

- Speed Penalty: None
- Purchase DC: 5 + one-half the mecha's base purchase DC

#### Duralloy Armor (PL 6)

Duralloy is harder, heavier, and more durable than alumisteel. It can also be used as a building material for mecha superstructures (see Mecha Superstructure).

- Equipment Bonus: +8
- Armor Penalty: -8
- Speed Penalty: -10 ft.
- Purchase DC: 10 + one-half the mecha's base purchase DC

#### Resilium Armor (PL 6)

Resilium is a more malleable alloy than duralloy, although not as strong.

- Equipment Bonus: +6
- Armor Penalty: -5
- Speed Penalty: None
- Purchase DC: 5 + one-half the mecha's base purchase DC

#### Crystal Carbon Armor (PL 7)

Grown in orbital laboratories, crystal carbon is a composite fiber material that narrowly outperforms neovulcanium on the battlefield.

- Equipment Bonus: +10
- Armor Penalty: -8
- Speed Penalty: None
- Purchase DC: 15 + one-half the mecha's base purchase DC

#### Neovulcanium Armor (PL 7)

Similar to duralloy, neovulcanium uses plasma-forging techniques to create an alloy of surprising resilience. It is also used as a building material for mecha superstructures (see Mecha Superstructure).

- Speed Penalty: -5 ft.
- Purchase DC: 10 + one-half the mecha's base purchase DC

### Mecha Equipment

Mecha equipment falls into several categories: flight systems, sensor systems, defense systems, weapons (both hand-held and integrated), and miscellaneous systems.

Installing a piece of equipment on a mecha, whether a weapon or another integrated system, requires a Craft (mechanical) check (DC 20).

| Mecha Size | Installation Time per Slot | Removal Time per Slot |
|---|---:|---:|
| Large | 10 minutes | 5 minutes |
| Huge | 30 minutes | 15 minutes |
| Gargantuan | 1 hour | 30 minutes |
| Colossal | 3 hours | 1.5 hours |

If a weapon or system occupies more than one equipment slot, multiply installation time by the number of slots it uses. An integrated weapon or system can be removed in half the installation time with a successful Repair check (DC 20).

#### Leave Room for the Pilot

When equipping a mecha from scratch, reserve at least two vacant equipment slots for the operator's cockpit. The cockpit cost is already included in the mecha's base purchase DC.

On Large mecha, the operator always occupies two of the following three slots:

- Helmet
- Torso
- Back

On Huge and larger mecha, the designer has more flexibility in cockpit placement. Copilot and passenger cockpits are described under Miscellaneous Equipment.

#### Equipment Entry Format

In addition to a general description, each equipment entry uses the following fields:

- Equipment Slots: The number of slots needed to install the equipment.
- Activation: How long it takes to activate the equipment (usually an attack action).
- Range or Range Increment: A listed range is maximum distance; a listed range increment follows standard range-increment rules.
- Maximum Range Increments: Unless otherwise noted, equipment with range increments has a maximum of ten increments.
- Target or Targets/Effect/Area: Describes whether the entry uses a target, effect, or area format and how it applies.
- Duration: How long the equipment operates once activated. Persistent duration means it remains active until the mecha is destroyed (0 hit points) or the operator turns it off.
- Saving Throw: The save type and result of a successful save, if any.
- Purchase DC: The Wealth check DC to acquire the equipment.
- Restriction: The license level required to purchase the equipment legally.

### Mecha Flight Systems

All mecha are equipped with legs that allow them to walk and run. A mecha's size determines its base speed, as noted in the Mecha Size table. This section describes optional flight systems.

To build a flight system from scratch, a character must succeed at a Craft (mechanical) check (DC 30) after investing 60 hours in assembly. A character without a mechanical tool kit takes a -4 penalty on the check. The character must also make a Wealth check against the system's purchase DC.

#### Afterburner System (PL 6)

The afterburner system dumps a shot of raw fuel into the mecha's thruster system, giving the mecha a temporary speed boost. The mecha's fly speed is doubled for 1 round. The system is good for one use; additional activations require additional afterburner systems.

- Equipment Slots: 1 (must be torso, back, or leg)
- Activation: Free action
- Range: Personal
- Target: You
- Duration: 1 round
- Saving Throw: None
- Purchase DC: 8 + one-quarter the mecha's base purchase DC
- Restriction: None

#### Jetpack (PL 6)

A jetpack combines vectored thrust with simple avionics, granting the mecha a fly speed of 100 feet (clumsy). The jetpack carries enough fuel for the mecha to travel a total of 1,000 feet; refueling has a purchase DC of 16.

- Equipment Slots: 1 (must be back or boots), or 2 (must be left leg and right leg)
- Activation: Free action
- Range: Personal
- Target: You
- Duration: Persistent
- Saving Throw: None
- Purchase DC: 10 + one-quarter the mecha's base purchase DC
- Restriction: None

#### Jet-Assist Wings (PL 7)

Jet-assist wings allow a flying mecha to maneuver more effectively, but do not provide the mecha with the ability to fly. A mecha with jet-assist wings improves its flight maneuverability by one category (clumsy to poor, poor to average, and so on).

- Equipment Slots: 1 (back or shoulders for Large or Huge), or 2 (back or shoulders for Gargantuan or Colossal)
- Activation: None
- Range: Personal
- Target: You
- Duration: Persistent
- Saving Throw: None
- Purchase DC: 5 + one-quarter the mecha's base purchase DC
- Restriction: None

#### Thruster Boots (PL 7)

Thruster boots combine powerful fusion thrusters to give the mecha a fly speed of 150 feet (poor).

- Equipment Slots: 1 (must be boots)
- Activation: None
- Range: Personal
- Target: You
- Duration: Persistent
- Saving Throw: None
- Purchase DC: 10 + one-quarter the mecha's base purchase DC
- Restriction: None

#### Ramjet Thruster Boots (PL 8)

This advanced thruster system uses ramjet technology to improve on PL 7 thruster boots, granting the mecha a fly speed of 200 feet (poor).

- Equipment Slots: 1 (must be boots)
- Activation: None
- Range: Personal
- Target: You
- Duration: Persistent
- Saving Throw: None
- Purchase DC: 10 + one-quarter the mecha's base purchase DC
- Restriction: None

### Mecha Sensor Systems

Sensor systems make it easier for mecha operators to perceive their surroundings. Not all mecha are equipped with sensors (or require them); without sensors, operators rely on normal vision and hearing.

A mecha with sensors conducts passive scans constantly in all directions, providing data on terrain, obstacles, and the location of nearby creatures, vehicles, and mecha.

A sensor system can also conduct an active scan of a single nonliving target (usually another mecha or vehicle). With a successful Computer Use check (DC 15) and a move action, the operator can actively scan and determine details based on the system used.

To build a sensor system from scratch, a character must succeed at a Craft (electrical) check (DC 30) after investing 60 hours in assembly. A character without an electrical tool kit takes a -4 penalty on the check. The character must also make a Wealth check against the sensor system's purchase DC.

Includes basic air/space radar for terrain and target acquisition. Grants a +2 equipment bonus on Navigate and Spot checks.

Active scan reveals:

- Target size
- Locomotive capabilities
- Present direction or trajectory

- Equipment Slots: 1
- Activation: Move action (active scan mode only)
- Range: 1-mile-radius emanation centered on your mecha
- Area: 1 nonliving target (active scan only)
- Duration: Persistent (passive) or 1 round (active)
- Saving Throw: None
- Purchase DC: 18
- Restriction: None

#### Class II Sensor System (PL 6)

Adds EM detection, infrared tracking, and high-resolution video with zoom. Grants a +2 equipment bonus on Navigate and Spot checks.

Active scan reveals everything from Class I, plus:

- Number of living creatures aboard (if applicable)
- Target hull or superstructure composition and armor type (if any)
- Target weapon systems (functional and nonfunctional)

- Equipment Slots: 1
- Activation: Move action (active scan mode only)
- Range: 1-mile-radius emanation centered on your mecha
- Area: 1 nonliving target (active scan only)
- Duration: Persistent (passive) or 1 round (active)
- Saving Throw: None
- Purchase DC: 21
- Restriction: None

#### Class III Sensor System (PL 6)

Combines Class II capabilities with advanced night vision. Grants darkvision 90 feet and a +2 equipment bonus on Navigate and Spot checks.

Active scan reveals the same information as Class II.

- Equipment Slots: 1
- Activation: Move action (active scan mode only)
- Range: 1-mile-radius emanation centered on your mecha
- Area: 1 nonliving target (active scan only)
- Duration: Persistent (passive) or 1 round (active)
- Saving Throw: None
- Purchase DC: 25
- Restriction: Licensed (+1)

#### Enigma Sensor Suite (PL 6)

Combines thermal imaging, X-rays, and vibration sensing to effectively see through solid objects. It cannot resolve fine detail.

A mecha with the Enigma suite reduces concealment by two grades (for example, total concealment 50% miss chance becomes three-quarters concealment 30% miss chance).

- Equipment Slots: 1
- Activation: None
- Range: 150 ft.
- Area: Cone-shaped emanation
- Duration: 1 round
- Saving Throw: None
- Purchase DC: 29
- Restriction: None

#### Oracle Targeting System (PL 6)

Computer-assisted targeting with holographic displays and predictive profiling. Grants an enhancement bonus on attack rolls when using one specific ranged weapon selected by the operator. Switching the bonus to a different weapon requires a move action.

- Equipment Slots: 1 (must be helmet or visor)
- Activation: Move action (activate or switch weapon)
- Range: Personal
- Target: You
- Duration: Persistent
- Saving Throw: None
- Restriction: None

Includes EM, infrared, hi-res video, night vision, and ladar for terrain/obstacle profiling. Grants darkvision 120 feet and a +4 equipment bonus on Navigate and Spot checks.

Upgrades to powerful multiband radar and stronger combined scanner arrays. Grants darkvision 180 feet and a +6 equipment bonus on Navigate and Spot checks.

- Target sensor systems (functional and nonfunctional)

- Equipment Slots: 1
- Activation: Move action (active scan mode only)
- Range: 10-mile-radius emanation centered on your mecha
- Area: 1 nonliving target (active scan only)
- Duration: Persistent (passive) or 1 round (active)

## Mecha Weapons

Mecha use both melee and ranged weapons to dispatch foes. A mecha weapon can be either handheld or integrated into the mecha's superstructure.

Sample mecha entries use the following fields:

- Size: The mecha's body size (and size penalty on attack rolls and Defense).
- Bonus Hit Points: Bonus hit points granted to the operator; also the mecha's total hit points.
- Superstructure: Primary material used to construct the mecha.
- Hardness: Damage reduction provided by the superstructure.
- Armor: Installed armor type.
- Bonus to Defense: Equipment bonus to Defense provided by armor.
- Armor Penalty: Penalty to Balance, Climb, Escape Artist, Hide, Jump, Move Silently, and Tumble checks.
- Reach: The mecha's reach.
- Strength Bonus: Equipment bonus to operator Strength.
- Dexterity Penalty: Penalty to operator Dexterity, if any.
- Speed: Base land speed and fly speed (if any).
- Base Purchase DC: Mecha base DC before armor/equipment.
- Standard Equipment Package: Baseline installed systems and weapons.

### Movement and Combat

Combat between mecha generally follows the normal combat rules for characters, with mecha-specific size, reach, speed, and equipment interactions layered on top.

Mecha still threaten squares within reach, take move and attack actions, use cover, and otherwise follow normal tactical rules. The following subsections describe special cases that arise during mecha operations.

[Return to Table of Contents](#table-of-contents)

### Cockpit Access

It takes a full-round action to climb into a mecha cockpit and a move action to activate systems so the mecha can move and fight.

Exiting a mecha normally takes a full-round action unless an HV-5 Haven escape pod or similar system is used.

[Return to Table of Contents](#table-of-contents)

### Driving, Piloting, and Movement

Mecha operators use Drive to operate mecha on the ground and Pilot to operate them in the air or space.

Normally, operators do not need Drive/Pilot checks to steer in combat. These skills matter in special cases:

- Move past a foe without provoking an attack of opportunity: use Drive or Pilot (as appropriate) instead of Tumble.
- Oppose a trip attempt: use Drive (ground) or Pilot (air).
- Recover from stall: successful Pilot check (see Flying Mecha).
- Copilot aid: a character in a copilot cockpit may use aid another with Drive or Pilot to assist the operator's checks.

[Return to Table of Contents](#table-of-contents)

### Flying Mecha

On the ground, mecha move like characters: they can turn freely, move in any direction, and stop quickly. In the air, movement is constrained by flight maneuverability.

Each flying mecha has a maneuverability rating determined by its flight systems.

#### Falling and Recovery

If a flying mecha fails to maintain minimum forward speed, it must land at the end of movement. If it is too high to land, it falls:

- First round: falls 150 feet.
- If still airborne: operator may attempt Pilot check DC 20 to recover.
- On failure: falls another 300 feet next round, then may attempt recovery again.
- If it hits the ground: it takes falling damage.

#### Flight Maneuverability Terms

- Minimum Forward Speed: Required speed to avoid forced landing/fall.
- Hover: Ability to remain stationary while airborne.
- Fly Backward: Ability to move backward.
- Reverse: Cost to begin flying backward.
- Turn: Turn angle after moving required distance.
- Turn in Place: Ability to pivot by spending movement.
- Maximum Turn: Maximum turn in a single space.
- Up Angle / Up Speed: Ascent angle and ascent rate.
- Down Angle / Down Speed: Descent angle and descent rate.
- Between Down and Up: Required level distance after descending before ascending.

| Maneuver | Perfect | Good | Average | Poor | Clumsy |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Minimum forward speed | None | None | Half | Half | Half |
| Hover | Yes | Yes | No | No | No |
| Fly backward | Yes | Yes | No | No | No |
| Reverse | Free | -5 ft. | - | - | - |
| Turn | Any | 90 degrees/5 ft. | 45 degrees/5 ft. | 45 degrees/5 ft. | 45 degrees/10 ft. |
| Turn in place | Any | +90 degrees/5 ft. | +45 degrees/5 ft. | No | No |
| Maximum turn | Any | Any | 90 degrees | 45 degrees | 45 degrees |
| Up angle | Any | Any | 60 degrees | 45 degrees | 45 degrees |
| Up speed | Full | Half | Half | Half | Half |
| Down angle | Any | Any | Any | 45 degrees | 45 degrees |
| Down speed | Double | Double | Double | Double | Double |
| Between down and up | 0 ft. | 0 ft. | 5 ft. | 10 ft. | 20 ft. |

### Mecha in Outer Space

Only mecha equipped with space skin can operate in outer space.

Unless the operator has Zero-G Training or the mecha has a zero-g stabilizer, the operator takes a -4 penalty on all attack rolls and skill checks in space.

In outer space, mecha flight differs from atmospheric flight in three ways:

- Maneuverability improves by one category (clumsy to poor, poor to average, average to good, etc.).
- Mecha may ascend/descend freely without speed penalties from atmospheric flight limits.
- Mecha can hover and do not need to maintain minimum forward speed.

### Mecha Critical Hits

When you confirm a critical hit against a mecha, you may roll percentile dice on the mecha critical table instead of applying normal critical multiplier damage. You must accept the rolled result.

| d% | Effect |
|---|---|
| 01-15 | Normal damage, crew dazed |
| 16-35 | Normal critical hit, crew dazed |
| 36-45 | Normal critical hit, mecha knocked prone |
| 46-50 | Severe critical hit, crew dazed, mecha stunned |
| 51-55 | Severe critical hit, mecha knocked prone |
| 56-60 | Crew hit (normal damage) |
| 61-70 | Normal damage, equipment damaged |
| 71-80 | Normal damage, equipment destroyed |
| 81-90 | Normal critical hit, slot damaged |
| 91-100 | Normal critical hit, slot destroyed |

#### Critical Hit Effects

- Normal Damage: Apply normal damage (no critical multiplier).
- Crew Dazed: Each crewmember (including operator) must succeed on Fortitude save DC 15 or be dazed 1 round.
- Normal Critical Hit: Roll critical damage normally.
- Mecha Knocked Prone: Mecha falls prone; all crewmembers/passengers take 1d6 bludgeoning damage. A prone mecha takes -4 on melee attacks, cannot use thrown ranged weapons, gains +4 Defense vs ranged and -4 Defense vs melee. Standing is a move action that provokes attacks of opportunity.
- Mecha Stunned: Mecha drops held items and cannot take move or attack actions for 1 round; operator Defense takes -2 while stunned.
- Severe Critical Hit: Roll critical damage using x5 multiplier instead of weapon's normal multiplier.
- Crew Hit: Normal damage is applied directly to one random crewmember/passenger, bypassing mecha bonus hit points.
- Equipment Damaged: One attacker-chosen system is damaged and nonfunctional until repaired (1 hour, Repair DC 20).
- Equipment Destroyed: One attacker-chosen system is destroyed and must be replaced.
- Slot Damaged: One attacker-chosen equipment slot is damaged; equipment in that slot cannot function until repaired (1 hour, Repair DC 25).
- Slot Destroyed: One attacker-chosen slot (and equipment in it) is destroyed; rebuilding requires 12 hours and Craft (mechanical) DC 30.

### Ranged Attacks and Attacks of Opportunity

When firing a ranged weapon in a threatened area, a mecha only provokes attacks of opportunity from creatures of its own size or larger (including other mecha).

[Return to Table of Contents](#table-of-contents)

### Stowing Hand Slot Equipment

Any equipment integrated into a mecha hand slot (and only hand slots) can be magnetically stowed against the mecha or in a storage compartment as a move action.

This frees the hand for fine manipulation (such as opening a door or pressing a control) or for grabbing another piece of mecha equipment.

A mecha may have more hand-slot equipment items than available hand slots, but it cannot use all of them simultaneously.
